## 1. Overview

This Sales Techniques training module equips Dayjoy distributors with the skills to confidently sell products, recruit new distributors, and build a sustainable direct selling business. The techniques covered are compliant with Dayjoy's Code of Conduct and India's Direct Selling Guidelines — **no high-pressure tactics, no false claims, no exaggerated income promises**.

### 1.1 Training Objectives

By the end of this training, you will be able to:

1. Master the 7-step Dayjoy sales process.
2. Handle the 10 most common objections effectively.
3. Use 5 proven closing techniques.
4. Build a sustainable warm-market and cold-market lead pipeline.
5. Recruit distributors ethically and effectively.

### 1.2 The Dayjoy Sales Philosophy

Dayjoy sales are built on **trust, not pressure**:

- ✅ **Listen first, sell second.** Understand the customer's need before recommending a product.
- ✅ **Recommend honestly.** If a product isn't right for the customer, say so.
- ✅ **Build relationships, not transactions.** A happy customer refers others; a pressured customer warns others.
- ✅ **Comply always.** No false claims, no exaggerated income, no high-pressure tactics.
- ❌ **Don't sell what you wouldn't buy yourself.**
- ❌ **Don't promise results you can't guarantee.**
- ❌ **Don't disappear after the sale.**

## 2. The 7-Step Dayjoy Sales Process

### Step 1: Prospecting

**Goal:** Identify potential customers and distributors.

#### Warm Market (Start Here)

- Family members
- Friends
- Colleagues
- Neighbors
- Members of clubs/associations you belong to
- Social media connections

#### Cold Market (Build Over Time)

- Local events (yoga classes, wellness fairs, community gatherings)
- Referrals from existing customers
- Social media (Facebook groups, Instagram, YouTube)
- Local business networking

#### Prospect Qualification

A qualified prospect has:

- A need your product can address (wellness, beauty, home care).
- Ability to pay (income level).
- Willingness to listen.
- Authority to make the purchase decision.

### Step 2: Approach

**Goal:** Make a positive first impression; build rapport.

#### Approach Principles

- **Smile and be genuine.**
- **Start with a question, not a pitch.** "How's your wellness routine going?" "Have you tried natural products before?"
- **Find common ground.** Family, work, hobbies, hometown.
- **Don't pitch on first contact (usually).** Build rapport first; pitch when invited.

#### Sample Approach Scripts

> **Approach 1 (Warm market — friend):**
> "Hi [Name]! I've started using some wellness products from Dayjoy and I'm really happy with them. I'd love to share what I'm using — would you be open to a quick chat sometime this week?"

> **Approach 2 (Warm market — family):**
> "Auntie, I noticed you've been talking about [concern — e.g., low energy]. I've been using a Dayjoy product that's been helpful for me. Can I tell you about it?"

> **Approach 3 (Cold market — event):**
> "Hi, I'm [Your Name]. I'm a Dayjoy wellness distributor. What brings you to this event today?"

### Step 3: Presentation

**Goal:** Show how your product addresses the customer's need.

#### Presentation Framework (FAB)

- **F (Features):** "This product contains [key ingredients]."
- **A (Advantages):** "It's formulated for [specific use / target group]."
- **B (Benefits):** "Which means it may help you [benefit relevant to customer's need]."

#### Presentation Tips

- Customize to the customer's stated need.
- Demonstrate the product (smell, touch, taste where appropriate).
- Share your personal experience or testimonial.
- Show the MRP and explain the value per use.
- Always include the compliance note ("wellness supplement, not a medicine; consult healthcare professional").

### Step 4: Trial Close

**Goal:** Test the customer's readiness to buy before final close.

A trial close is a low-pressure question that gauges interest:

- "How does this sound to you?"
- "Would this fit into your routine?"
- "Do you see how this could help with [their need]?"
- "Is this something you'd like to try?"

If the customer says yes → move to Step 6 (Close).
If the customer hesitates → move to Step 5 (Objection handling).

### Step 5: Objection Handling

**Goal:** Address the customer's concerns honestly and respectfully.

#### Objection Handling Framework (LAER)

- **L (Listen):** Let the customer fully explain their concern.
- **A (Acknowledge):** "I understand your concern." "That's a fair question."
- **E (Explore):** "Could you tell me more about what specifically worries you?"
- **R (Respond):** Address the specific concern with information, not pressure.

#### The 10 Most Common Objections

See Section 3 below for detailed objection handling.

### Step 6: Close

**Goal:** Confirm the sale and arrange payment + delivery.

#### Closing Techniques (Section 4)

- Assumptive close
- Alternative close
- Summary close
- Urgency close (use sparingly and honestly)
- Action close

### Step 7: Follow-Up

**Goal:** Ensure customer satisfaction; build long-term relationship; request referrals.

#### Follow-Up Schedule

- **24 hours after delivery:** "Did you receive the product? Any questions?"
- **7 days after delivery:** "How are you finding the product? Any feedback?"
- **30 days after delivery:** "Would you like to reorder or try another product?"
- **60 days after delivery:** "Do you know anyone else who might benefit?"
- **Quarterly:** Stay in touch with useful content, not just sales pitches.

## 3. The 10 Most Common Objections — Handling Playbook

### Objection 1: "It's too expensive."

**LAER Response:**

- **L:** "I understand. Let me help you see the value."
- **A:** "Price is a fair concern. Let me show you something."
- **E:** "What's your current monthly spend on [category]?"
- **R:** Show value per use. Compare to alternatives. Offer combo discount. Mention satisfaction guarantee.

**Sample Response:**

> "I understand price is important. Let me show you the value per use: this [Product] lasts [X] weeks with daily use, costing just ₹[Y] per day — less than your daily tea! Plus, if you're not satisfied, we have a [return window] return policy. Would you like to try it with that assurance?"

---

### Objection 2: "I've never heard of Dayjoy."

**Sample Response:**

> "That's completely understandable — Dayjoy doesn't spend on big advertising, which keeps our prices fair. We've been around since 2018, based in Kota, Rajasthan, and operate in 9 Indian states. Our model is direct selling — meaning customers buy from distributors like me instead of retail stores. The money saved on middlemen goes into product quality and distributor income. Would you like to hear more about the company?"

---

### Objection 3: "Does it really work?"

**Sample Response:**

> "I appreciate you asking. Wellness products work differently for everyone — there's no 'one size fits all.' I personally use [Product] for [benefit] and have had a positive experience. Many of our customers share similar feedback. That said, results depend on consistency, lifestyle, and individual factors. We have a satisfaction guarantee — if you don't find it helpful, you can return it within [return window]. Would you like to try it with that assurance?"

---

### Objection 4: "I need to think about it."

**Sample Response:**

> "Of course — it's important to make the right decision. May I ask what specifically you'd like to think about? Sometimes there's an underlying concern I can address right now."

(If they share the concern → address it. If not →)

> "That's fine. I'll check back in a couple of days. In the meantime, here's a brochure with all the product details. Does [day/time] work for me to call you back?"

---

### Objection 5: "I'm already using another brand."

**Sample Response:**

> "That's great that you're using something! I'm not here to switch you if you're happy. But would you be open to trying one product as a comparison? Many of our customers say [specific benefit] is what they noticed. If you don't see a difference, you can return it within [return window]. Fair?"

---

### Objection 6: "Is this a pyramid scheme?"

**Sample Response:**

> "Great question — and one I'm glad you asked. Pyramid schemes are illegal in India, and Dayjoy is a legitimate direct selling company registered with the government. Here's the difference:
>
> - **Pyramid schemes** make money from recruitment fees — no real products. We have real wellness products in 12 categories.
> - **Pyramid schemes** require big joining fees. Dayjoy distributor onboarding is **free** — no joining fee.
> - **Pyramid schemes** don't buy back unsold inventory. We have a buyback policy at 90% of price.
> - **Pyramid schemes** promise guaranteed income. We disclose average earnings and emphasize effort-based income.
>
> You can verify Dayjoy's registration with the Consumer Affairs Department in [your state]. Would you like me to share more details?"

---

### Objection 7: "I don't have time for this."

**Sample Response:**

> "I totally get it — life is busy. I'll respect your time. Can I leave this brochure with you? If you ever want to try a product, my contact is on it. No pressure at all."

(Sometimes a low-pressure approach opens the door later.)

---

### Objection 8: "My doctor said to avoid supplements."

**Sample Response:**

> "I completely respect your doctor's advice — they know your health best. Please follow their guidance. If your doctor is open to specific natural wellness products, I'd be happy to share the ingredient list for them to review. Until then, let's focus on other products they've approved — like our home care or personal care range."

---

### Objection 9: "I'll buy online instead (Amazon/Flipkart)."

**Sample Response:**

> "I understand the convenience. However, please note that Dayjoy products sold on Amazon/Flipkart by unauthorized sellers may be counterfeit, expired, or improperly stored. The only authorized online source is `dayjoy.in` or my distributor portal. Plus, when you buy from me, you get personal support, product guidance, and a satisfaction guarantee. Would you like to place your order with me directly?"

---

### Objection 10: "Let me ask my spouse/family."

**Sample Response:**

> "Absolutely — family decisions are important. Would it help if I gave you a one-page summary you can share with them? Or, if they have specific questions, I'd be happy to chat with both of you together. What works best?"

## 4. Closing Techniques

### 4.1 Assumptive Close

Assume the customer has decided and move to logistics:

> "Great — shall I have this delivered to your home address or office?"

### 4.2 Alternative Close

Give two options, both of which result in a sale:

> "Would you prefer the small size or the family pack?"

### 4.3 Summary Close

Summarize the benefits agreed upon, then ask for the sale:

> "So we've covered that [Product] helps with [Benefit 1], is suitable for your [specific need], comes with a satisfaction guarantee, and costs just ₹[amount]. Would you like to go ahead?"

### 4.4 Urgency Close (Use Honestly)

Genuine urgency only — never fake scarcity:

> "The current promotional offer ends on [date]. After that, the price goes up by ₹[amount]. Would you like to lock in the current price?"

### 4.5 Action Close

Simply ask for the sale:

> "Would you like to place an order today?"

## 6. Recruiting Distributors

### 6.1 Who to Recruit

Ideal distributor prospects:

- Entrepreneurial mindset.
- Part-time income need (homemakers, students, retirees, side-hustlers).
- Wellness enthusiasts.
- Strong social networks.
- Coachable and willing to learn.
- Located in serviceable states.

### 6.2 Recruiting Process

1. **Identify** — Within your network, who has entrepreneurial interest?
2. **Approach** — "I've been building a wellness business with Dayjoy. It might interest you too — can I share what I'm doing?"
3. **Share your story** — Why you joined, what you've achieved, what you've learned.
4. **Present the opportunity** — Use the official Dayjoy presentation. Emphasize:
   - Free onboarding
   - Multiple income streams
   - Training and support
   - 24/7 AI assistant
5. **Address concerns** — Especially pyramid scheme concern (Section 3, Objection 6).
6. **Help them register** — Online via your distributor link.
7. **Onboard them** — Walk them through the Distributor Onboarding training.

### 6.3 Required Disclosures When Recruiting

When presenting the opportunity, you must:

- Disclose your name, distributor ID, contact.
- Provide the official Dayjoy business plan document.
- Disclose average earnings: "In 2025, the average annual income for an active Dayjoy distributor was ₹[X]. See dayjoy.in/income-disclosure. Results not typical."
- Disclose that income depends on effort and results vary.
- Disclose the buyback policy for unsold inventory.
- Provide the Grievance Officer's contact.
- Disclose that there is no mandatory product purchase to join.
- Provide cooling-off period information.

### 6.4 Building Your Team

- **Set expectations early.** New distributors should know it takes 6–12 months to see consistent income.
- **Be available.** Respond to your downline's questions within 24 hours.
- **Train, don't just recruit.** A trained distributor stays; an untrained one leaves.
- **Recognize achievements.** Celebrate every rank advancement, every milestone.
- **Lead by example.** Don't ask your downline to do what you're not doing.

## 7. Sales Ethics & Compliance

### 7.1 What NOT to Do

- ❌ Don't make medical claims about products.
- ❌ Don't promise specific income amounts.
- ❌ Don't use high-pressure tactics ("limited time only," "buy now or miss out").
- ❌ Don't target minors (under 18) for recruitment.
- ❌ Don't sell on Amazon/Flipkart without authorization.
- ❌ Don't make false comparisons with competitors.
- ❌ Don't use before/after photos without substantiation.
- ❌ Don't harass prospects who said no.
- ❌ Don't misrepresent your relationship with Dayjoy (you're an independent distributor, not an employee).

### 7.2 What TO Do

- ✅ Use the official product information from the Distributor Portal.
- ✅ Share your honest personal experience.
- ✅ Disclose income per the Income Disclosure Statement.
- ✅ Provide Grievance Officer contact to dissatisfied customers.
- ✅ Respect "no" — leave the door open for future.
- ✅ Follow up consistently but respectfully.
- ✅ Continuously improve your product and sales knowledge.
- ✅ Treat your Dayjoy business like a real business — because it is.
