## 1. Overview

Dayjoy Marketing Private Limited operates as a **direct selling company** in India and is subject to the regulatory framework governing direct selling in India. This document outlines the legal framework, Dayjoy's compliance obligations, distributor conduct rules, and consumer protection requirements.

### 1.1 Why This Document Matters

Direct selling in India has historically faced regulatory ambiguity, with confusion between legitimate direct selling and illegal money circulation schemes (pyramid schemes). The **Direct Selling Guidelines** issued by the Government of India on **9 September 2016** provided the first comprehensive regulatory framework. Compliance with these guidelines is **non-negotiable** — non-compliance can result in:

- State-level bans on operations.
- Criminal prosecution under the **Prize Chits and Money Circulation Schemes (Banning) Act, 1978 (PCMC Act)**.
- Consumer Protection Act proceedings.
- Loss of distributor network trust.
- Reputational damage.

### 1.2 Governing Laws

Dayjoy's direct selling operations are governed by:

| Law / Guideline | Year | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Selling Guidelines** | 2016 (9 September) | Central framework for direct selling entities |
| **Consumer Protection Act** | 2019 | Consumer rights and dispute redressal |

## 3. Pyramid Scheme vs. Legitimate Direct Selling — Distinction

### 3.1 What is a Pyramid Scheme (Banned under PCMC Act)?

A pyramid scheme is characterized by:

1. **Compensation based primarily on recruitment** rather than product sales.
2. **Required inventory loading** — distributors must buy large inventory to participate.
3. **No buyback policy** for unsold inventory.
4. **Entry fees** — high fees to join.
5. **Misleading income claims**.
6. **No genuine product** or products with inflated prices.
7. **Geometric progression earnings** (promising exponential income through recruitment).

### 3.2 Dayjoy's Compliance with Anti-Pyramid Rules

| Rule | Dayjoy Compliance | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Compensation based on product sales, not recruitment | ✅ Compensation plan rewards retail sales (MRP minus DP) + team business volume (BV) — not recruitment alone | VERIFIED |
| No mandatory inventory loading | ✅ Free distributor onboarding; no mandatory product purchase to join | VERIFIED |
| Buyback policy for unsold inventory | ✅ Buyback at ≥90% of original price | VERIFIED |
| No entry fees | ✅ Free distributor registration | VERIFIED |
| Truthful income disclosure | ⚠️ Income Disclosure Statement required — [PLACEHOLDER — client to publish IDS] | REQUIRES CLIENT INPUT |
| Genuine products at fair prices | ✅ Products sold at MRP across categories | VERIFIED |

### 3.3 Dayjoy's Compensation Plan Compliance

**VERIFIED:** Dayjoy's compensation plan includes:

- **Retail profit** (up to 30% — MRP minus DP) — tied to actual product sales, not recruitment.
- **Performance incentive** — based on Business Volume (BV) from product sales, not recruitment count.
- **Binary structure** with caps on payouts — prevents geometric progression earnings.
- **Rank advancement** tied to BV + PV (sales-based metrics), not recruitment count alone.

The compensation plan is published in the official Compensation Plan PDF and on the website.

## 4. Distributor Conduct Rules (Consumer Protection Rules, 2021)

### 4.1 Prohibited Distributor Practices

Distributors must NOT:

1. **Make false or misleading representations** about product quality, benefits, or income potential.
2. **Use unfair trade practices** including bait-and-switch, false advertising, deceptive pricing.
3. **Engage in coercion** — pressure, harass, or threaten prospective customers or recruits.
4. **Make exaggerated income claims** — must disclose average earnings per Income Disclosure Statement.
5. **Target vulnerable populations** — minors (under 18), elderly with cognitive decline, etc.
6. **Misrepresent the business opportunity** — must clearly state it requires effort and results vary.
7. **Recruit from other direct selling companies** (cross-recruitment) without their consent.
8. **Sell on unauthorized e-commerce platforms** (Amazon, Flipkart) without Dayjoy's permission.
9. **Make medical claims** about products without regulatory approval.
10. **Conduct pyramid scheme practices** — compensation based on recruitment, inventory loading.

### 4.2 Required Distributor Disclosures

When recruiting new distributors, current distributors must:

1. Disclose their name, distributor ID, and contact information.
2. Provide the Dayjoy business plan document.
3. Disclose average earnings (per Income Disclosure Statement).
4. Disclose that income depends on effort and results vary.
5. Disclose the buyback policy for unsold inventory.
6. Provide the Grievance Officer's contact information.
7. Disclose that there is no mandatory product purchase to join.
8. Provide a cooling-off period of [PLACEHOLDER — typical: 30 days] during which the new distributor can exit with full refund.

## 5. Consumer Protection Requirements

### 5.1 Consumer Rights (Consumer Protection Act, 2019)

Dayjoy customers have the right to:

1. **Be informed** — about product quality, price, ingredients, side effects.
2. **Choose** — without coercion or pressure.
3. **Be heard** — grievances addressed within 30 days.
4. **Seek redressal** — through Consumer Forum if not satisfied.
5. **Consumer education** — Dayjoy publishes product information transparently.

### 5.2 Mandatory Disclosures to Consumers

Dayjoy must disclose:

1. Company name, registered address, GSTIN, contact details.
2. Product information: ingredients, MRP, manufacturing date, expiry date, batch number.
3. Return, refund, and cancellation policies (see `support/return-policy.md`).
4. Warranty terms (see `support/warranty-policy.md`).
5. Total price (including all taxes and charges).
6. Delivery timeline and shipping charges (see `support/shipping-policy.md`).
7. Grievance Officer contact details.
8. Dispute resolution process.

### 5.3 Prohibited Product Claims

Dayjoy and its distributors must NOT make:

- **Medical claims** without CDSCO approval (e.g., "cures cancer," "treats diabetes").
- **Exaggerated benefit claims** (e.g., "miracle cure," "100% results").
- **Misleading comparisons** with competitors.
- **False scarcity claims** (e.g., "only 5 left" when ample stock exists).
- **False testimonials** or unverified before/after photos.
- **Bait advertising** (offering a product with no intention to sell at advertised price).

## 10. Advertising Compliance

### 10.1 ASCI (Advertising Standards Council of India)

All Dayjoy advertising must comply with ASCI codes:

- **Truthful and honest** — no misleading claims.
- **Not offensive** — to public decency or morality.
- **Not harmful** — to consumers or competitors.
- **Fair in competition** — no unfair comparative ads.
- **Compliant with law** — including consumer protection law.

### 10.2 Required Disclosures in Advertising

- **Income claims:** Must include "Average distributor earnings: ₹X/year. Results not typical. See dayjoy.in/income-disclosure."
- **Health claims:** Must include "This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease. Consult your healthcare professional."
- **Material connections:** Testimonials must disclose if the person was compensated.
- **Sponsored content:** Must be tagged #ad / #sponsored per ASCI influencer guidelines.

### 10.3 Prohibited Advertising

- Ads targeting minors (under 18) for distributor recruitment.
- Ads making unsubstantiated medical claims.
- Ads promising guaranteed income.
- Ads using religious or political imagery.
- Comparative ads naming competitors.
