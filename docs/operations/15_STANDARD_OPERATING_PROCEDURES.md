# Dayjoy Enterprise AI Platform — Standard Operating Procedures (SOP)

> **Purpose**
>
> Define the complete Enterprise Standard Operating Procedures framework that governs how routine operational activities are documented, executed, reviewed, standardized, and continuously improved across the organization.

---

## 1. SOP Framework Overview

### 1.1 Purpose

The SOP framework provides Dayjoy with a structured method for turning recurring work into reliable, repeatable, and measurable procedures. SOPs reduce variation, improve quality, support onboarding, and make routine operational work easier to execute consistently.

### 1.2 SOP Role

SOPs are the operational memory of the organization. They define how work should be performed, who should perform it, what standards apply, and how exceptions should be handled.

### 1.3 Operational Context

Dayjoy is an enterprise AI platform with business, technical, operational, security, and governance activities that must be repeated reliably. SOP governance ensures those recurring activities are standardized across the organization and maintained over time.

SOP governance and documentation guidance from enterprise and technical sources emphasizes clear ownership, structured templates, version control, training linkage, periodic review, and lifecycle management to keep procedures accurate and usable. [589][590][591][594][595][596][597][598][599][603]

---

## 2. Objectives

The SOP framework is intended to:

- Standardize recurring operational work.
- Improve quality and consistency.
- Reduce dependency on individual memory or informal knowledge.
- Support training, onboarding, and cross-team continuity.
- Improve compliance and audit readiness.
- Make exceptions and deviations visible.
- Create a living process library that improves over time.

---

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 Included Scope

The framework covers:

- SOP governance.
- SOP classification.
- SOP lifecycle management.
- SOP creation, review, approval, and versioning.
- SOP execution standards.
- SOP training and adoption.
- SOP compliance monitoring.
- SOP documentation and quality standards.
- SOP KPI tracking and continuous improvement.

### 3.2 Excluded Scope

## 4. SOP Principles

| Principle | Description | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| Standardization | Common work should follow common procedures | Reduces variation |
| Clarity | SOPs should be easy to understand and follow | Improves adoption |
| Ownership | Every SOP should have a responsible owner | Improves accountability |
| Currency | SOPs should reflect current reality | Prevents drift |
| Practicality | SOPs should be usable in real work | Improves compliance |

| Improvement | SOPs should evolve as the organization changes | Supports maturity |

SOP governance guidance consistently recommends clear style, concise writing, ownership, periodic review, and linkage to actual work execution so procedures do not become stale artifacts. [591][594][595][596][598][599][603]

## 5. SOP Governance

### 5.1 Governance Purpose

SOP governance defines the rules, roles, review cadence, and control model used to manage SOPs as enterprise assets.

### 5.2 Governance Structure Model

| Body / Function | Role |
|---|---|
| SOP Governance Board | Oversees SOP standards and exceptions |
| SOP Owner | Owns the content and relevance of a SOP |
| Process Owner | Owns the business process described by the SOP |
| Quality Reviewer | Validates clarity, consistency, and correctness |
| Compliance Reviewer | Reviews policy or regulatory impact |
| Training Coordinator | Connects SOP changes to adoption and training |
| Document Controller | Manages publishing, versioning, and archive status |

### 5.3 Guidance

- Governance should include both process and document control.
- High-impact SOPs should receive stronger review.
- SOP governance should be operational, not just administrative.

Enterprise SOP governance guidance recommends structured governance boards, backup ownership, role clarity, and periodic review cycles with clear revision and approval procedures. [595][596][597][598][599]

---

## 6. Roles & Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| SOP Owner | Owns SOP accuracy, relevance, and maintenance |
| Process Owner | Owns the underlying business or operational process |
| Subject Matter Expert (SME) | Provides expert input and validation |
| Reviewer | Reviews for clarity and correctness |
| Approver | Authorizes publication or major revision |
| Document Controller | Manages version control and publication |
| Training Coordinator | Aligns SOP updates with training and adoption |

### 6.1 Responsibility Guidance

- Every SOP should have a named primary owner and backup owner.
- Owners should be accountable for keeping SOPs current.
- Approval authority should match the SOP’s risk and importance.

SOP governance best practices emphasize primary ownership, backup continuity, SMEs for technical accuracy, approvers for effective dates, and training coordination after changes. [595][596][597][598][599]

## 7. SOP Classification

### 7.1 Classification Purpose

Classifying SOPs helps the organization apply the right level of review, training, and monitoring.

### 7.2 Classification Model

| Class | Description | Typical Control Level |
|---|---|---|
| Standard SOP | Routine, stable, repeatable procedure | Regular review |
| Critical SOP | High-impact or business-critical procedure | Strong review and training |
| Operational SOP | Day-to-day operational process | Scheduled review |
| Compliance SOP | Procedure tied to policy or regulatory needs | Formal review and approval |
| Emergency SOP | Procedure used in urgent situations | Tight governance and periodic practice |

### 7.3 Guidance

- SOP class should influence review frequency and approval depth.
- Critical and compliance-related SOPs should receive more scrutiny.
- Classification should be visible in the document metadata.

SOP governance guidance recommends risk tiers or criticality classes to define different review and approval chains for different procedures. [595][597][598][599][603]

## 8. SOP Lifecycle

### 8.1 Purpose

The SOP lifecycle ensures procedures are created, validated, published, maintained, and retired in a controlled manner.

### 8.2 Lifecycle Stages

| Stage | Description |
|---|---|
| Identification | A recurring activity is identified for formal documentation |
| Drafting | The SOP is created or updated |
| Review | SMEs and stakeholders validate content |
| Approval | Authorized approver signs off |
| Publication | Current version is made available |
| Training | Relevant users are informed and trained |
| Monitoring | Compliance and usefulness are tracked |
| Review / Update | SOP is refreshed on schedule or on change |
| Retirement | SOP is removed or archived when no longer needed |

### 8.3 Guidance

- SOPs should be treated as living assets.
- Review frequency should reflect risk and change rate.
- Retired SOPs should not remain active as if current.

SOP governance best practices emphasize lifecycle management from drafting through retirement, with periodic review and archive control. [595][596][598][599][603]

## 9. SOP Creation Process

### 9.1 Purpose

The SOP creation process defines how a new SOP is developed and approved.

### 9.2 Creation Steps

1. Identify the recurring activity.
2. Assign ownership.
3. Define scope and purpose.
4. Map the actual process.
5. Draft the SOP in the standard format.
6. Review with SMEs and stakeholders.
7. Revise based on feedback.
8. Approve and publish.
9. Train affected users.
10. Review adoption and effectiveness.

### 9.3 Guidance

- SOPs should describe real work, not idealized work only.
- Drafting should involve the people who actually perform the process.
- The initial version should be practical and usable.

SOP best-practice guidance recommends collaborative development, process mapping, clear roles and responsibilities, and validating actual versus expected work before publication. [591][595][597][603]

---

## 10. SOP Review & Approval Process

### 10.1 Purpose

Review and approval ensure SOPs are accurate before they become active.

### 10.2 Review Stages

- SME review.
- Process owner review.
- Compliance or quality review where needed.
- Approval by authorized owner or manager.
- Publication after approval.

### 10.3 Guidance

- Review should be based on SOP criticality.
- Significant changes should receive deeper review.
- Approval should be tied to effective date and version.

SOP governance guidance recommends clear approval workflows and defined approver authority, especially for higher-risk or regulated procedures. [595][596][597][598][599]

---

## 11. SOP Version Management

### 11.1 Purpose

Version management ensures it is clear which SOP is current and how it has changed over time.

### 11.2 Standards

- Each SOP should have a version identifier.
- Significant content changes should result in a new version.
- Minor editorial changes should still be traceable.
- Previous versions should be archived appropriately.

### 11.3 Guidance

- Versioning should support auditability and user clarity.
- Current SOP status should be obvious to readers.
- Uncontrolled copies should be minimized.

SOP governance best practices recommend explicit versioning systems, archived prior versions, and controlled publication of the current version in a centralized repository. [595][596][598][599][603]

---

## 12. SOP Execution Standards

### 12.1 Purpose

Execution standards ensure SOPs are followed consistently in real operations.

### 12.2 Standards

- SOPs should be used as the authoritative reference for routine work.
- Deviation should be handled only when appropriate and documented.
- Staff should know where to find the current SOP.
- SOPs should be written so they can be followed reliably.

### 12.3 Guidance

- SOPs should be written in clear, action-oriented language.
- Steps should be logically ordered and easy to follow.
- Exceptions should be described where necessary.

Technical and enterprise SOP guidance recommends concise language, clear layouts, accessible repositories, and testing SOPs with less experienced staff to ensure they can be followed with minimal variation. [591][594][595][596][597][603]

---

## 13. SOP Training & Adoption

### 13.1 Purpose

Training and adoption ensure staff know about SOPs and can apply them correctly.

### 13.2 Guidance

- SOP updates should be communicated to affected users.
- Training should be linked to critical SOP changes.
- Onboarding should include relevant SOP awareness.
- Adoption should be measured where important.

### 13.3 Why It Matters

A well-written SOP that no one uses does not improve operations.

SOP governance guidance recommends integrating SOP updates with training assignments, acknowledgment records, and ongoing adoption support. [591][594][595][596][598][599]

---

## 14. SOP Compliance Monitoring

### 14.1 Purpose

Compliance monitoring checks whether SOPs are being followed in practice.

### 14.2 Guidance

- Compliance should be monitored for critical SOPs.
- Deviations should be investigated and corrected.
- Monitoring should feed into governance and improvement.

### 14.3 Why It Matters

SOPs must reflect how work is actually performed; otherwise, governance becomes theoretical.

SOP governance guidance recommends monitoring SOP usage and compliance, and reporting when necessary so organizations can see whether documented processes are being used effectively. [591][594][595][596][599]

---

## 15. SOP Documentation Standards

### 15.1 Purpose

Documentation standards ensure SOPs are uniform, understandable, and maintainable.

### 15.2 Standards

- SOPs should use a common style and format.
- The purpose, scope, responsibilities, and procedure should be clear.
- Language should be simple and active.
- SOPs should be easy to locate and use.

### 15.3 Guidance

- Use consistent headings and structure.
- Keep procedures as concise as practical.
- Avoid ambiguity and unnecessary complexity.

SOP and documentation guidance consistently emphasizes standardized style, easy navigation, and clear sectioning so procedures are accessible and easy to maintain. [590][591][594][595][596][598][603]

---

## 16. SOP Quality Assurance

### 16.1 Purpose

Quality assurance ensures SOPs are accurate, usable, and fit for purpose.

### 16.2 QA Focus

- Accuracy.
- Completeness.
- Clarity.
- Currency.
- Consistency.
- Usability.

### 16.3 Guidance

- SOPs should be tested with real users where appropriate.
- Issues should be corrected before publication or re-publication.
- Quality checks should be part of the SOP lifecycle.

SOP best-practice guidance recommends validation through testing and user review, because clarity and practical usability are essential for SOP effectiveness. [591][595][597][603]

## 17. SOP KPIs

### 17.1 KPI Catalog

| KPI | Description |
|---|---|
| SOP Coverage | How many recurring activities have SOPs |
| Review Timeliness | How consistently SOPs are reviewed on time |
| Approval Completion Rate | How often SOP approvals are completed successfully |
| SOP Adoption Rate | How often SOPs are used in operations |
| Compliance Rate | How often SOPs are followed |
| SOP Freshness | How current SOPs remain |
| SOP Quality Score | How usable and clear SOPs are |

### 17.2 Guidance

- KPIs should reflect both documentation quality and operational use.
- Metrics should be reviewed by governance and process owners.
- Poor adoption or stale content should trigger improvement.

## 18. Continuous SOP Improvement

### 18.1 Improvement Goals

- Improve clarity and usability.
- Reduce inconsistency and duplication.
- Keep SOPs current.
- Improve adoption and compliance.

### 18.2 Guidance

- SOPs should be updated as work changes.
- Feedback from users should be considered.
- SOPs should be retired when they are no longer needed.

SOP governance guidance recommends regular review cycles, backlog management, and ongoing monitoring of usage and relevance to keep procedures effective over time. [595][596][598][599]
